  • Credit cost per closed loan ranges from $326 (Olmsted) to $870 (Harold). The driver is how many pulls it takes to close a loan, not the price per pull.
  • Biggest lever: investor free credit pulls. Olmsted moved to an investor's free pull option over the summer. His credit cost fell from $471 to $117 per closed loan (Section 5).
  • A standard tri-merge costs $89, including joint reports. Harold's soft pulls cost $75 single / $150 joint, more than a standard pull.

  • With investor free pulls (Olmsted, June–September), the per-loan figure falls to about $347 ($117 + $230).

1. Method

  • Source: credit vendor monthly statements for Joe Olmsted, Joseph Carey and Harold Ellis (two accounts: standard and soft pull). Every month was reconciled to the vendor's own monthly summary totals.
  • RMCR $250 charges are excluded. They are recouped from the borrower at closing, so they are not a net cost to the company. Each RMCR line corresponds to a closed loan, and the RMCR counts closely match Salesforce closings, which supports the closing counts used here.
  • Payments are not costs. Invoice payments and autopay lines are excluded.
  • Cost includes add-ons such as rescores, report copies, tradeline updates and upgrades. A "pull" is a billed credit report (any bureau combination).
  • Closed loans come from Salesforce: transactions where the LO is the assigned Loan Officer, with a closing date in the month and a status of Closing, Funding, PC Review or Post-Closed.
  • Period: January–September 2026 (9 full months). October is partial and is left out of the averages.

Data notes

  • Carey, July: the second half of the July statement is missing from the PDF; about $698 of charges is estimated.
  • Small samples: Carey has 18 closings and Harold 30 over nine months, so one or two loans move their per-loan figures noticeably.
  • Timing: pulls and closings are matched by calendar month. A pull in one month often belongs to a loan that closes one to two months later, so nine-month totals are more reliable than any single month.

4. Soft-pull accounts: should new outside LOs get one?

Harold is the only LO with both a soft-pull and a standard-pull account, so his data shows what soft pull does in practice.

Finding (2026 statements)Result
Price of a soft pull vs. a standard tri-merge$75 single / $150 joint vs. $89
Borrowers soft-pulled106
… who later also got a standard pull (paid twice)34 (32%)
Borrowers standard-pulled154
… who had a soft pull first40 (26%)
Pulls per closed loan: Harold vs. Olmsted / Carey9.4 vs. 3.6 / 4.3
Credit cost per closed loan: Harold vs. Olmsted / Carey$870 vs. $326 / $440
  • Soft costs more, not less. At these prices every soft pull costs about the same as or more than going straight to a standard pull, so it cannot save money even when it screens out a borrower.
  • About a third are paid twice. When a soft-pulled borrower moves forward, the file still needs a standard pull for underwriting.
  • It isn't used as a screen. Three quarters of Harold's standard pulls had no soft pull first, so the soft account adds volume instead of replacing hard pulls.
Recommendation: do not give soft-pull accounts to new outside loan officers at current pricing. Revisit only if the vendor offers a soft product priced well below the $89 standard pull. One single-bureau soft pull on Harold's account was billed at $24.75, which shows cheaper options exist. If soft pull is ever rolled out, use company-controlled sub-accounts that can be audited and switched off, since every pull still requires a permissible purpose and borrower authorization.

5. Investor free credit pulls

Over the summer Olmsted began using an investor's free credit pull option. His billed pulls fell from about 40 a month to 2 by September, while his closings held steady.

OlmstedBilled pulls / monthCredit cost / monthClosed loansCredit cost per closed loan
January–May (paid pulls)37.6$3,39336$471

Recommendation: make the investor free-pull option the default for every LO on eligible loans, including new hires. If Carey and Harold matched Olmsted's summer rate, their combined credit spend would fall from about $3,780 a month to about $625. The fallback is the $89 standard pull through the company account, not a soft pull.

Because Olmsted's free pulls do not appear on the vendor statements, his nine-month average ($326) blends both periods. Use $471 as the paid-pull rate and $117 as the free-pull rate.
